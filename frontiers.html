STRUCTURED EXCITATION + INTERFEROMETRIC DETECTION

Two complementary ways to encode position.

φ = 0π/2π3π/2

Known phases change expected photon counts. A molecule’s position sets the phase of the response; noisy counts constrain that position.

Transparent measurement & estimation model

Three independent channel banks allocate one third of the signal to x, y and z. This deliberate abstraction is not the paper’s camera/PSF model. Each channel count is Poisson distributed.

Enhanced channels: μj = (900/12)[1 + m cos(2πu/P + φj)] + 3. Lateral P = 400 nm, m = 0.75; axial P = 300 nm, m = 0.80. Summing four phases preserves the expected photon budget.

Conventional channels use broad calibrated spatial gradients: μj = (900/12)[1 ± u/L] + 3, with L = 240 nm laterally and 600 nm axially. These are coarse coordinate readouts, not actual interferometric phases or a pixelated conventional PSF.

The estimator minimizes Σ(μ − n log μ) on a 1 nm grid, then refines the minimum. It receives counts, mode and calibration only. Local σ = 1/√Σ[(∂μ/∂u)²/μ], evaluated at the estimate. No feature switch assigns an error directly.

The problem

In conventional 3D localization, a molecule’s image often encodes its axial coordinate less strongly than its lateral position. More magnification does not supply the missing positional information.

The innovation

Structured excitation changes detected counts with lateral position. Fluorescence collected by opposing objectives interferes, making detection sensitive to axial phase. Combining the two adds complementary information.

01

What students should remember

Localization precision describes the spread of repeated position estimates. Spatial resolution describes the ability to distinguish structures. Label size, sampling density, drift and systematic errors also matter; one accurately localized molecule does not establish image resolution.

02

What the researchers found

Wang and colleagues combine structured illumination with dual-objective coherent fluorescence detection and report near-isotropic 2–3 nm localization precision. This is a published experimental result, not a guaranteed simulator output.

03

Limitations

Experimental precision depends on phase stability, optical calibration, labelling and photon statistics. These constraints are idealized here.

Known brightness/background, perfect phase calibration, one stationary molecule, independent channels, no bleaching, dipole effects, drift, aberrations or detection-efficiency differences. Equal photon budgets isolate information encoding.

Phase ambiguity & scoring

The search assumes a known coarse cell: x,y within ±140 nm and z within ±100 nm. These ranges span less than one enhanced period. Without a coarse prior, periodic responses admit aliases; this simulator does not solve global fringe-order ambiguity.

After ≥12 trials per mode, combined lateral and axial uncertainty must each be <75% of conventional. A separate simulator-only audit also requires position RMSE on both axes <80% of conventional and no combined estimates at a search boundary. Only then is truth displayed. The audit never alters estimates. Finite repeats fluctuate; repeat batches if needed.

Local Fisher ellipses may be unreliable near bounds or for non-Gaussian likelihoods. This is not the full experimental 4Pi-SIMFLUX reconstruction pipeline.
