01

The ideal width relationship

d = d₀ / √(1 + I/Iₛₐₜ)

We set d₀ = λ/(2NA) = 228.6 nm using λ = 640 nm and NA = 1.40, then use d as the FWHM of a Gaussian effective PSF. The lecture’s resolution scaling is therefore a calibrated width proxy here, not an exact PSF calculation.

02

Scan, then judge the image

Each pixel is the sum of the two emitters’ effective PSF responses at that scan position. The sample is fixed, 128 nm apart. The completed central line must contain a local peak on each side and a valley ≤73.5% of the weaker peak.

This is an explicit contrast-based teaching criterion, not the Rayleigh criterion for Gaussian spots or a universal resolution standard.

03

What the model leaves out

Ideal central zero, exact alignment, stationary identical point emitters, noiseless detection, no bleaching or aberrations. PSF peak stays normalized; detected counts and dose are not calibrated.

Doughnut versus effective PSF

The red beam is an illustrative r² exp(−r²/r₀²) doughnut with r₀ = 160 nm. I/Iₛₐₜ is the effective depletion-strength parameter in the width law; we do not solve rate equations from this displayed ring. The Gaussian suppression corresponds to a near-axis parabolic depletion approximation. It is not a full pulsed or continuous-wave STED simulation.

Image and beam scales are fixed during adjustment. Doughnut brightness is normalized to slider maximum; fluorescence images share a fixed 0–2 signal scale. Reference dots are schematic. Lecture 4 pp. 17–21 supplies the STED principle, phase plate and resolution relationship.

Confocal and STED: nuclear pore labels

  • Change the fluorescent region: stimulated emission suppresses fluorescence outside the depletion beam’s dark centre, narrowing the effective fluorescence PSF.
  • More power is not a free improvement: dose, bleaching, alignment and the quality of the central zero matter. This image does not calibrate our depletion slider.
