Select a layer. Encode detail. Reconstruct.

This diagram illustrates the published LA-SIM concept. It is separate from the optical-sectioning experiment above and does not generate reconstructed data.

The problem

A thick living cell produces out-of-focus background. Acquiring many patterned frames also costs time and light dose; motion between frames can undermine reconstruction.

The innovation

A lattice light sheet selectively activates a layer of switchable labels. Structured illumination reads that layer out, and computation combines the encoded frames. Activation, excitation and reconstruction play different roles.

01 · Selective activationChoose which layer can fluoresce

A lattice light sheet activates reversibly photoswitchable fluorophores in a selected layer. Activation changes their fluorescent state; it is distinct from exciting fluorescence during readout.

Known illumination patterns excite the activated fluorophores. Multiple pattern measurements encode spatial information for SIM reconstruction. The paper explores linear and nonlinear schemes; these stripes are a conceptual symbol, not a simulated acquisition.

Why the sheet removes haze

We sum fluorescence from fixed structures at three depths. A Gaussian illumination envelope weights each emitter: L(Δz) = exp[−4 ln 2 · (Δz/t)²], where t is sheet FWHM. Broad illumination uses L = 1. Out-of-focus sources remain in the image but become broader through the detection PSF.

Model assumptions and units

All lengths are µm. The lateral Gaussian detection width is σ(Δz) = 0.12√[1 + (Δz/0.6)²]; each emitter’s PSF is normalized to preserve its weighted flux before cropping. Both views share a fixed linear intensity scale and equal peak excitation, not equal total dose. Out-of-focus fraction counts modeled detected intensity from sources farther than 0.35 µm from focus.

This is a noiseless scalar teaching model with discrete source planes, no scattering, aberrations, bleaching, photoactivation dynamics, lattice side lobes or deconvolution. Preset widths are teaching values, not specifications from the paper.

What the researchers found

Published experimental finding: Dong and colleagues demonstrate volumetric super-resolution imaging by combining lattice-sheet activation, structured readout and learning-based processing. The study explores linear and nonlinear schemes, beyond optical sectioning alone.

What students should remember

The sheet chooses the layer; patterned measurements encode detail; reconstruction estimates the structure. Our workbench demonstrates only depth-dependent excitation and a stack of acquired slices. It does not simulate activation kinetics or LA-SIM reconstruction.

Limitations

Switchable labels constrain performance and colour choices. Light dose, motion, optical calibration and the reliability of computational processing must be considered. Learned processing requires validation on the relevant specimen and acquisition conditions. No published performance value is promised by this sectioning simulator.
