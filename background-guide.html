Try this Keep separation at 300 nm. Increase NA until the Rayleigh criterion is met. Then increase magnification: does the verdict change?

The model & its limits

dR = 0.61λ / NA. The plotted profile is [2J₁(u)/u]², where u = 2πNA·r/λ; the two incoherent intensities add. A circular, aberration-free pupil, identical emitters and no noise are assumed. Rayleigh is a conventional criterion, not a universal yes/no boundary. Real images also depend on sampling, background, aberrations and contrast. The display does not simulate a complete microscope. Source: L3 pp. 32–40; Nikon MicroscopyU.

Interpretation & limitations

Spherical and chromatic aberrations, source size, drift, specimen thickness, scattering and dose all matter. Higher voltage reduces wavelength but can increase some damage mechanisms; it is not automatically the best choice. This is a signal-path diagram and relativistic wavelength calculator, not a Monte Carlo scattering simulation. Conventional TEM contrast depends on imaging conditions; it is not a direct height map. EXPERIMENT 03

A tip changes the story.

AFM measures tip–surface interactions. STM measures a distance-sensitive tunnelling current.

SURFACE PROFILEIdealised model

Try this Increase the tip radius. Does the object become wider or taller? Switch to STM and increase the gap by 0.1 nm.

The models & their limits

AFM uses z(x) = maxᵤ[h(u) − (x − u)²/(2R)] for a rigid parabolic tip, a 10 nm-high, 20 nm-wide rectangular ridge, and a flat substrate. This geometric envelope ignores deformation, adhesion and feedback dynamics. STM uses I/I₀ = exp[−2κ(z − z₀)], with κ = √(2mₑφ)/ℏ, φ = 4 eV and z₀ = 0.5 nm. Fixed bias and constant electronic density of states are assumed. Real STM contrast depends on electronic structure as well as geometry. Source: L6 pp. 4, 12–22, 26–39.

EXPERIMENT 04

Overlap carries information.

In ptychography, neighbouring probe positions share specimen regions. This redundancy helps constrain a computational reconstruction.

A fixed 5 × 5 grid is shown. Step = probe diameter × (1 − overlap). This percentage describes linear overlap, not the shared area of two circles.

OVERLAPPING PROBE POSITIONSAcquisition geometry

Interpret carefully More overlap at fixed probe size and fixed field of view requires more positions, increasing dose and acquisition time if dose per position stays fixed.

What this demo does—and does not—calculate

The circles show scan geometry only. No diffraction data are measured, no phase retrieval runs, and no resolution improvement is calculated. A real 4D-STEM dataset records a 2D diffraction pattern at each 2D scan position. Reconstruction fits a physical model to these measurements. Success depends on dose, detector sampling, coherence, drift and model accuracy—not overlap alone. Read the research context below.

2021 / MILESTONE

When the specimen becomes the limit

Chen and colleagues reported instrumental blurring below 20 pm using multislice electron ptychography. Atomic-column widths were then dominated by thermal motion rather than instrument blur. This is a reported result for specific experimental conditions, not a universal specification.

2026 / RECENT DEVELOPMENT

Learning from correlated atomic motion

Gladyshev and colleagues introduced CAVIAR, combining mixed-object ptychography with analysis of displacement correlations. Simulations and an hBN experiment supported extracting information about correlated vibrations. This extends the question from “where are the atoms?” to “how is their motion related?”

Read the Nature Communications paper ↗
A question worth asking

If two reconstruction models explain the same measured intensities, what independent evidence would help distinguish them? Consider repeat scans, dose dependence, reference materials and agreement with complementary measurements.

STED

A depletion beam suppresses emission around an excitation spot, shrinking the effective fluorescent region. Higher depletion intensity brings photophysics and dose trade-offs.

STORM / PALM

Separate sparse emitters in time, localise each one, then combine localisations. Single-emitter precision is not the same as the resolution of a densely labelled structure.

SIM

Known illumination patterns shift otherwise inaccessible spatial information into the detected range. Conventional linear SIM offers roughly a twofold lateral improvement under suitable conditions.
