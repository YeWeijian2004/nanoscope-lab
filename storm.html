01

Sparsity separates signals

A single fluorophore makes a broad diffraction-limited spot. Its center can be estimated more precisely than its width. Nearby simultaneous emitters combine into a spot that may yield a misleading center.

In this model each molecule has an independent probability of being bright in every exposure. This approximates photoswitching without molecular dwell-time kinetics or bleaching.

02

What the localizer knows

The algorithm receives only camera pixels. It smooths to find peaks, then estimates a background-subtracted weighted centroid in a 15 × 15 pixel window. Signal and width checks reject obvious blends; subtle overlaps can still pass.

Numerical model

48 fixed emitters; Gaussian PSF σ = 100 nm; 1,200 expected photons per bright molecule per exposure; Poisson photon counts plus 2 background photons/pixel. Detection uses a 3 × 3 mean filter and peaks ≥6 photons/pixel. Candidates within 200 nm are merged; accepted windows require 700–1,650 background-subtracted counts and moment width 70–115 nm. This is a simple centroid model, not a validated maximum-likelihood fitter.

03

Coverage is not enough

The reconstruction draws every accepted estimate, including mistakes. More points do not automatically mean a better image: excessive activation can create biased estimates and false structures.

Transparent challenge scoring

A separate simulator audit matches estimates one-to-one to currently bright molecules within 40 nm. Coverage counts distinct molecules successfully matched; quality is matches / all estimates. Neither scoring nor reference positions adjusts the reconstructed points. Real experiments lack this ground-truth score and need independent quality assessment.

Fixed noiseless molecular positions, ideal optics, no drift, bleaching, molecular interactions or detector read noise. Display scales are fixed per panel: camera 0–30 photons, conventional 0–100. FROM SIMULATION TO REAL MICROSCOPY

STORM maps microtubules and tau

  • Notice two labelled distributions: filament-like microtubules and concentrated tau signal. Colour overlap alone does not prove a molecular interaction.
  • Separate in time: sparse emission lets a localizer estimate individual spot centres. Accumulating coordinates reveals structures whose signals would overlap under dense simultaneous emission.
  • Precision is only part of the story: labelling density, drift, repeated blinking and localization errors affect the reconstructed structure. Our centroid filter is an educational approximation.
