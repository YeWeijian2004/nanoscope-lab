Actual numerical unmixing

At each pixel: y = A x + ε, where each row of A contains the four responses at one acquired potential and x contains four non-negative label intensities. We minimize ‖Ax − y‖² subject to x ≥ 0 by evaluating all active subsets of four components. Only captured arrays and their response calibration enter the solver.

Rank and condition number are computed from A. The condition number is the ratio of largest to smallest singular value; large values amplify noise. The threshold of 20 is a teaching choice. Repeated identical potentials are not counted as new information.

The problem

Spectrally overlapping labels can emit into the same detection band. One intensity image then mixes their contributions, even if the STED image is spatially sharp.

The innovation

Potential-dependent fluorescence adds a new contrast dimension. Calibrated response profiles and sufficiently independent measurements let a constrained solver separate non-negative label contributions.

Synthetic measurements

Four partially overlapping biological-style structures share the same fixed Gaussian image blur. Observations are their weighted sum plus Gaussian noise (σ = 0.01 intensity units). Known response profiles are 0.06 + 0.94/[1 + exp(−(V−V₀)/0.07)], with V₀ = −0.55, −0.25, +0.05, +0.25 V.

These illustrative, memoryless curves are not experimental dye data. No real dye names or quantitative STED resolution are assigned. The preview shows the expected mixture; acquired thumbnails include noise. Display scales are fixed, with separate labelled scales for mixtures and components.

What the researchers found

The paper demonstrates separating highly overlapping fluorophores through distinct electrochemical fluorescence profiles and constrained linear unmixing, including four-colour STED with a single-colour optical configuration.

What students should remember

Electrochemical contrast distinguishes labels; it does not improve STED’s intrinsic spatial resolution. False colours are assigned only after computational separation.

Limitations

The reported setup uses an ITO electrode with a redox-mediated buffer; the demonstrated modulation is limited to fixed, permeabilized cells. Real calibration depends on dye chemistry, environment, electrode control, kinetics and photobleaching.

This model excludes those effects, hysteresis, motion and calibration error. Completion requires informative measurements and a relative fit residual below 10%; a good fit alone does not certify correct biological labelling.

Mixture scale: 0–2 intensity units; recovered components: 0–1. The solver never uses the specimen arrays to recover or score a component.

Optional further reading: Yang et al., Electrochemical fluorescence modulation enables simultaneous multicolour imaging, Nature Photonics (2025). DOI: 10.1038/s41566-025-01672-7.
